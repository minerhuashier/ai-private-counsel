# AI 帮我打官司，最强私人律师上线

## 一个普通人的十二个月：从“我该去哪个法院”到可核验的 AI 自诉工作流

> 公开稿，待本人审阅。文中人物、平台、商家、法院和案件细节均已隐去或泛化。
>
> 这不是“AI 替我打赢了官司”的故事。AI 不能替我签字、缴费、提交材料，也不能保证任何案件结果。它做的，是帮助我把混乱变成问题，把问题变成证据，把证据变成可以复核的材料。

### 一、那十二个月，我不是什么都没做

事情的开始并不复杂。

我购买了一项有时间要求的服务。付款前，对方确认可以按约定提供。临近履行时，对方却告诉我，原来的方案做不了，只能换成另一种安排。

我没有接受替代方案。

但事情没有因此回到原点。服务没有按照原约定发生，钱却没有回来。后来，我和平台反复沟通，提交情况，希望平台在事实没有解决之前先处理这笔交易。等待我的不是明确答案，而是一层层转接、一次次说明、一个个“请继续等待”。

那时候我最真实的感受不是愤怒，而是不知道从哪里开始。

我知道这件事不对。可当“我觉得不公平”要变成一个可以提交给法院的问题时，中间隔着太多东西：应该去哪个法院？该告谁？案由是什么？聊天记录怎么整理？录音怎么用？法条去哪里找？起诉状写到什么程度才能提交？

我打开搜索框，搜了很多问题，也看了很多互相矛盾的答案。有人说先投诉，有人说直接起诉，有人说找商家，有人说找平台。每一条看起来都有道理，但我仍然不知道自己明天早上应该先做什么。

那十二个月里，我并不是什么都没做。我保留了聊天，保存了付款记录，也反复尝试沟通。但我没有把这些材料变成一条能够被别人检查的路径。

我只是一个普通人，第一次认真面对“自己去法院维护权利”这件事。

### 二、我第一次问 AI 的，不是“帮我写诉状”

我最早问 AI 的问题很朴素：

> “遇到这种事情，我应该去哪个法院？接下来到底该怎么办？”

我没有期待它立刻给我一份完美文书。我当时真正需要的，是有人先告诉我：这件事可以从哪里拆开。

AI 没有直接给我一个可以照抄的答案。它先追问了几个我当时没有认真想过的问题：

- 付款是向谁付的？
- 实际提供服务和收款的是不是同一个主体？
- 交易页面、订单规则和平台协议分别写了什么？
- 我认为平台有责任，依据是它知道什么、做了什么，还是只是没有达到我的期待？
- 哪些事实有材料，哪些只是我的记忆？
- 我请求返还多少钱，依据是什么，计算方式是什么？

这些问题没有让案件立刻变简单，却让我第一次把“我很委屈”改成了“我需要证明哪些事实”。

后来我才明白，这是 AI 在这件事里最早的真正价值。

它不是先替我写一段像法律文书的话。它先帮我把一团情绪，拆成一串可以回答的问题。

### 三、AI 写得很像，不等于写得正确

继续往下做，AI 很快展现出另一面。

它可以几分钟内生成一段结构完整、语气专业的分析；可以给出法条名称、条号和看起来非常合理的论证；也可以在事实还没有完全核实时，写出一份像模像样的起诉状。

问题是，法律工作不是作文比赛。

一个条号错了，整段论证就可能失效。一个责任主体选错了，法院可能要求重新起诉。一个看似确定的事实没有证据支撑，写得越流畅，风险越大。

所以我后来给自己定了几条很不“智能”、但非常重要的规则：

1. **没有证据编号的事实，只能算待核实。**
2. **没有官方法律原文支持的法条和程序结论，不进入正式文书。**
3. **原始材料只读保存，转写、截图和整理件不能覆盖原件。**
4. **AI 可以起草，但签字、缴费、提交和公开发布只能由我本人决定。**
5. **来源冲突时先停下来核对，不为了让文章完整而猜一个答案。**

这些规则后来变成了项目里的协议：防止幻觉、证据固定、来源核验、隐私最小化、人工批准和公开安全。

它们不是为了让 AI 显得更聪明，而是为了让它在该停下来的时候真的停下来。

### 四、第一步不是写起诉状，是建立证据台账

真正开始推进以后，我才知道，起诉状不是起点。

起点是材料。

我把聊天记录、付款凭证、订单页面、客服沟通、录音、短信和后续收到的程序通知分别保存。原件不改名、不裁剪、不覆盖；整理件另存；每一份重要材料记录来源、时间和用途。

然后，AI 帮我把材料转成文本，按日期、主体和行为排成时间线。

这个过程看起来不精彩，却改变了很多事情：

- 我能看到同一件事在不同时间有哪些不同说法；
- 我能区分客观发生的事、对方的陈述、我的感受和我的法律主张；
- 我能发现“我记得有，但材料里没有”的空白；
- 我也能发现“材料里有，但诉请里没有对应”的遗漏。

以前我是在讲一个故事。后来我是在维护一张能回到原件的证据网。

这两件事看起来相似，实际完全不同。

### 五、第一次提交失败，反而救了我

第一次提交材料时，我以为最大的困难已经过去了。

结果，法院退回了申请。

当时的挫败感很强。我第一反应是：是不是起诉状写得不够好？是不是再加几段论证就可以？

后来才发现，问题不在文笔，而在更前面。

我把不同的法律关系混在了一起，也把责任主体想得过于简单。文字可以润色，路径错了却不能靠润色修好。

那次退回迫使我重新做三件事：

1. 重新确认我到底依据哪一类法律关系主张权利；
2. 重新确认谁应当是被告，谁的行为与这项请求直接对应；
3. 重新检查每一项诉请是否有事实、证据和法律依据。

AI 在这个过程中继续帮我对照版本、列出矛盾、查找来源，但最后的选择必须由我自己作。

我删过请求，换过被告，也重写了不只一版材料。最开始觉得“少写一项就是吃亏”，后来才明白，写下一项自己无法证明的请求，才是真正的风险。

### 六、从“写一份文书”到维护一套状态

案件进入法院程序后，问题又变了。

以前我以为，材料提交完就只剩等待。实际上，期限会变化，程序会调整，平台页面会更新，短信和正式文书也可能不是同一时间到达。

“已经缴费”和“法院系统已经显示入账”不是同一个状态。

“法院已经接收证据”和“法院已经采信证据”不是同一个状态。

“对方暂时没有回应”和“对方承认责任”更不是同一件事。

所以我不再只问 AI：“现在是什么情况？”

我开始要求它回答：

- 这条信息来自哪里？
- 页面上是什么时间显示的？
- 有没有更晚的正式通知？
- 这份材料证明的是发生过什么，还是只证明某个页面当时这样写？
- 如果两个来源冲突，哪一个更正式、更新？

从那以后，AI 在我的工作里不再只是一个“写手”。它更像一个记录员、校对员和提醒器，帮我在不断变化的程序状态里，分清哪些是事实，哪些只是页面状态，哪些还需要本人核对。

### 七、今天，我仍然不敢说“赢了”

今天，案件仍在法院程序中。

已经完成了一些节点：材料提交过，补正处理过，调解尝试过，案件后来正式进入诉讼程序，也收到过新的程序通知和费用通知。

但这些都不是胜诉。

立案不是胜诉。法院接收证据不是采信。进入下一阶段不是支持我的请求。对方暂时没有提交材料，也不等于对方承认责任。

我越来越觉得，普通人用 AI 维权时，最重要的能力不是让 AI 说出一个“肯定能赢”的答案，而是能够接受它说：

> “这里还不能确定。”

> “这个来源不够正式。”

> “这项事实没有证据。”

> “这个动作必须由你本人决定。”

AI 不会因为我焦虑就替我承担后果。法院也不会因为一份文书语言流畅就跳过证据。

所以，“AI 私人律师”如果真要成立，它不能是一个什么都敢回答的聊天框。

它应该是一套有边界的系统：

- 原件先于叙述；
- 证据先于结论；
- 官方来源先于模型记忆；
- 人工批准先于外部动作；
- 无法核验时，宁可停止；
- 任何结果，都不提前宣布胜利。

### 八、我为什么还叫它“最强私人律师”

不是因为它从不犯错。

恰恰相反，是因为它终于开始被要求承认自己会犯错。

它不能代替执业律师，不能保证案件结果，也不能替一个普通人承担签字和提交的责任。

但它可以让我这样的普通人，在面对一套陌生程序时，不再只有“忍着”或者“到处问人”两个选择。

它可以帮我把材料找齐，把时间线排清，把法条核到原文，把文书改到能逐项解释，把每一个“我觉得”追到“证据在哪里”。

对我来说，这才是“AI 帮我打官司”的真实含义。

不是 AI 站上法庭替我说话。

而是当我一个人坐到电脑前，不知道下一步该做什么时，我终于有了一套能开始、能停下、能核对、也能由我自己负责的办法。

### 九、如果你也正准备开始

如果你也遇到类似的事，我希望你先记住几件不那么热血、但可能真正有用的事：

1. **先保存原件。** 不要为了整理而覆盖截图、录音、付款记录和原始通知。
2. **先写时间线。** 只写时间、人物、行为和来源，不急着写谁“可恶”。
3. **把事实、感受和主张分开。** 三者都真实，但在法律材料里不是同一种东西。
4. **不要相信 AI 给出的条号、案号和案例。** 回到发布机关、法院或官方的原文核验。
5. **不要把所有个人信息一次性交给任何 AI。** 先脱敏，再讨论方法。
6. **AI 可以帮你起草，但不能替你决定、签字、缴费、提交或发布。**
7. **如果案件复杂、金额较大或后果重大，及时咨询执业律师。** AI 的作用是辅助整理，不是取消专业帮助。

我还在走这条路，案件也还没有最终结果。

但和十二个月前相比，我至少已经知道：面对一件不知道怎么办的事，最重要的不是找到一个永远不会错的答案，而是建立一套能够让错误被发现、让事实被核对、让每个动作都有人负责的流程。

这，就是我的 AI 自诉助手。

它不是替我赢官司的机器。

它是在我最不知道该往哪里走的时候，帮我把下一步变得可以检查的一盏灯。

### 说明与边界

本文是一名普通当事人的个人经历和方法复盘，不是对任何具体案件的法律意见，也不构成对案件结果的预测。为避免给当事人、商家、平台工作人员和司法机关带来不必要的影响，文中没有公开真实姓名、平台或商家主体、法院名称、案号、金额、日期、联系方式、证据编号和可供反查的过程细节。

案件仍在法院程序中，本文不宣称胜诉，也不把立案、缴费、证据接收、程序转换或任何单个程序节点描述为实体裁判结果。遇到复杂、金额较大或后果重大的纠纷，应及时咨询具备执业资格的律师。

# AI Helps Me Fight My Case: The Ultimate Private Counsel Is Online

## One Ordinary Person's Twelve Months: From "Which Court Should I Go To?" to a Verifiable AI-Assisted Self-Litigation Workflow

> Public draft for review. Names of people, platforms, businesses, courts, and case details have been omitted or generalized.
>
> This is not the story of AI winning a case for me. AI cannot sign for me, pay for me, file materials for me, or guarantee any outcome. What it did was help me turn confusion into questions, questions into evidence, and evidence into materials that could be reviewed.

### 1. During Those Twelve Months, I Was Not Doing Nothing

The beginning was not complicated.

I purchased a service that had to be performed within a fixed time window. Before I paid, the provider confirmed that it could be delivered as agreed. Close to the performance date, I was told that the original arrangement could not be carried out and that I would have to accept a different one.

I did not accept the substitute.

But the matter did not return to its original state. The service was not performed as agreed, and the money did not come back. I communicated with the platform repeatedly and submitted the facts, hoping that the transaction would be handled before the underlying dispute was resolved. Instead of a clear answer, I received transfers between departments, repeated requests for explanation, and one "please continue to wait" after another.

What I felt most strongly at the time was not anger. It was not knowing where to begin.

I knew something was wrong. But between "I feel this is unfair" and "this is a question I can submit to a court" stood a long list of unknowns: Which court should I go to? Whom should I sue? What should the cause of action be? How should chats be organized? How can a recording be used? Where should I find the actual text of a law? How complete must a complaint be before it can be filed?

I searched for many of these questions and found many answers that contradicted one another. Some said to complain first. Some said to sue immediately. Some said to pursue the business. Others said to pursue the platform. Each answer sounded reasonable, but I still did not know what I should do first the next morning.

During those twelve months, I was not doing nothing. I kept the chats, saved the payment records, and tried to communicate again and again. But I had not turned those materials into a path that another person could inspect.

I was simply an ordinary person facing the prospect of protecting my own rights in court for the first time.

### 2. The First Thing I Asked AI Was Not "Write a Complaint for Me"

My first question was very simple:

> "In a situation like this, which court should I go to? What am I supposed to do next?"

I did not expect a perfect legal document immediately. What I needed was someone to help me see where the problem could be broken apart.

AI did not hand me an answer that I could copy without thinking. It first asked questions that I had not seriously considered:

- To whom did I make the payment?
- Were the entity providing the service and the entity receiving the payment the same?
- What did the transaction page, order rules, and platform agreement each say?
- Did I believe the platform was responsible because of what it knew, what it did, or simply because it had not met my expectations?
- Which facts were supported by materials, and which existed only in my memory?
- How much was I asking to recover, on what basis, and how was that amount calculated?

Those questions did not make the case simple. They did something more useful: they changed "I feel wronged" into "what facts do I need to prove?"

Only later did I understand that this was AI's first real contribution.

It did not start by writing a paragraph that sounded like a legal document. It first helped me turn a mass of emotion into a series of answerable questions.

### 3. AI Can Sound Right Without Being Correct

As the work continued, I saw another side of AI.

It could produce a structurally complete and highly professional analysis within minutes. It could provide names and numbers of legal provisions and an argument that looked entirely reasonable. It could even produce a convincing-looking complaint before the facts had been fully verified.

The problem is that legal work is not a writing contest.

One wrong article number can invalidate an entire argument. One wrong responsible party can force the case to be started again. One supposedly certain fact without evidentiary support becomes more dangerous when it is expressed in smoother language.

So I later established several rules that were not especially "intelligent," but were essential:

1. **A fact without an evidence ID is only a fact awaiting verification.**
2. **A legal provision or procedural conclusion without support from an official text does not enter a formal draft.**
3. **Original materials are preserved read-only; transcripts, screenshots, and working copies must never overwrite them.**
4. **AI may prepare a draft, but signing, payment, filing, and public release remain my decisions.**
5. **When sources conflict, stop and verify rather than inventing an answer to make the document look complete.**

Those rules later became project protocols: anti-hallucination, evidence preservation, source verification, privacy minimization, human approval, and public-safety review.

They were not designed to make AI appear smarter. They were designed to make it stop when it should stop.

### 4. The First Step Was an Evidence Ledger, Not a Complaint

Once I began to make real progress, I learned that the complaint was not the starting point.

The materials were.

I preserved the chats, payment records, order pages, customer-service communications, recordings, messages, and procedural notices separately. I did not rename, crop, or overwrite the originals. Working copies were stored separately. For every important item, I recorded its source, time, and intended use.

AI then helped me turn the materials into text and arrange them into a timeline by date, actor, and action.

The process looked unremarkable, but it changed several things:

- I could see where the same event had been described differently at different times;
- I could distinguish what objectively happened, what another party said, what I felt, and what I argued legally;
- I could identify gaps between "I remember this happened" and "the materials show it happened";
- I could identify omissions where the evidence existed but no corresponding request had been made.

At first, I was telling a story. Later, I was maintaining a network of evidence that could always be traced back to an original.

The two may look similar, but they are fundamentally different.

### 5. My First Failed Filing May Have Saved Me

When I submitted the materials for the first time, I thought the hardest part was over.

Then the filing was returned.

The frustration was intense. My first reaction was to ask whether the complaint had not been written well enough, or whether adding several more arguments would fix it.

Later, I realized that the problem was not the prose. It was earlier in the chain.

I had mixed different legal relationships together and had treated the question of the responsible party too simply. Wording can be polished. A wrong path cannot be repaired by polishing.

The return forced me to redo three things:

1. Reconfirm the legal relationship on which my claim was based;
2. Reconfirm who should be the defendant and whose conduct corresponded directly to each request;
3. Recheck whether every request had a factual, evidentiary, and legal basis.

AI continued to help me compare versions, identify contradictions, and find sources, but the final choices had to be mine.

I removed requests, changed the defendant, and rewrote the materials more than once. At first, I thought omitting a request meant giving something up. Later, I understood that the greater risk was to include a request I could not prove.

### 6. From "Writing a Document" to Maintaining a State

Once the case entered the court process, the problem changed again.

Previously, I thought that once the materials were submitted, all I could do was wait. In reality, deadlines change, procedures are adjusted, platform pages update, and messages and formal documents may arrive at different times.

"The fee was paid" and "the court system shows the payment as credited" are not the same state.

"The court received evidence" and "the court accepted the evidence" are not the same state.

"The other side has not responded yet" and "the other side has admitted liability" are certainly not the same thing.

So I stopped asking AI only, "What is the situation now?"

I began to require answers to these questions:

- Where did this information come from?
- What time was displayed on the page?
- Is there a later formal notice?
- Does this material prove that something happened, or only that a page displayed a particular status at a particular time?
- If two sources conflict, which one is more formal and more recent?

From that point on, AI was no longer just a writer in my workflow. It became more like a record keeper, proofreader, and reminder that helped me separate facts from page states and page states from matters that still required personal verification.

### 7. Today, I Still Cannot Say That I Have Won

Today, the case is still proceeding through the court.

Some procedural milestones have been completed: materials were submitted, corrections were handled, mediation was attempted, the case later entered formal litigation, and additional procedural and fee notices were received.

None of that is a victory on the merits.

Formal case acceptance is not victory. Receipt of evidence is not acceptance of evidence. Moving to the next procedural stage is not a ruling in my favor. The other side's failure to submit materials so far is not an admission of liability.

The more I use AI in this process, the more I believe that the most important ability for an ordinary person is not getting AI to say, "You will definitely win." It is being able to accept answers such as:

> "This cannot be determined yet."

> "This source is not sufficiently authoritative."

> "There is no evidence for this fact."

> "This action must be decided by you."

AI does not bear the consequences simply because I am anxious. A court will not skip the evidence because a document reads smoothly.

So if the term "AI private counsel" is to mean anything, it cannot describe a chat box that answers everything with confidence.

It should be a system with boundaries:

- Originals before narrative;
- Evidence before conclusions;
- Official sources before model memory;
- Human approval before external action;
- Stopping when verification fails;
- Never announcing victory before a result exists.

### 8. Why I Still Call It My "Strongest Private Counsel"

Not because it never makes mistakes.

Quite the opposite: it is because it is finally required to acknowledge that it can make mistakes.

It cannot replace a licensed lawyer, guarantee an outcome, or assume responsibility for an ordinary person's signature and filing decisions.

But it can give an ordinary person like me something more than "endure it" or "ask around" when facing an unfamiliar legal process.

It can help me find the materials, organize the timeline, check legal provisions against official texts, revise a document until each section can be explained, and trace every "I think" back to "where is the evidence?"

That is what "AI helps me fight my case" means to me.

It does not mean that AI stands in court and speaks for me.

It means that when I sit alone at a computer and do not know what to do next, I finally have a method that can begin, stop, verify, and remain my own responsibility.

### 9. If You Are About to Begin

If you are facing a similar situation, I hope you will remember a few things that are not dramatic but may be genuinely useful:

1. **Preserve the originals first.** Do not overwrite screenshots, recordings, payment records, or original notices in the name of organization.
2. **Write a timeline first.** Record the time, the person or entity involved, the action, and the source. Do not rush to describe who is "evil."
3. **Separate facts, feelings, and claims.** All three can be real, but they are not the same kind of material in a legal process.
4. **Do not trust article numbers, case numbers, or cases cited by AI without verification.** Return to the issuing authority, court, or official text.
5. **Do not hand all of your personal information to any AI at once.** Minimize and redact before discussing method.
6. **AI may help you draft, but it cannot decide, sign, pay, file, or publish for you.**
7. **If the case is complex, the amount is significant, or the consequences are serious, consult a licensed lawyer promptly.** AI assists with organization; it does not eliminate the need for professional help.
